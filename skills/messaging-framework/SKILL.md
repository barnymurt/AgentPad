# Messaging Framework

Create a coherent messaging framework that defines how to talk about your product across all channels and audiences. Unlike generic marketing copy, this skill produces structured messaging based on your specific product, target market, and competitive positioning — ensuring consistency from your website to sales conversations to press releases.

## Core Workflow

### Step 1: Understand the Product and Market

Before creating messaging, understand what you're selling and to whom:

1. **Product definition:**
   - What is the product? (core functionality)
   - What problem does it solve?
   - What's the key differentiator?

2. **Target audience:**
   - Who is the primary buyer/user?
   - What are their goals?
   - What are their pain points?

3. **Competitive context:**
   - Who are the alternatives?
   - What's the differentiation?
   - What claims are hard to make?

If the user hasn't provided this context, ask clarifying questions. Generic messaging won't work.

### Step 2: Develop Value Proposition

Craft the core message that drives all other messaging:

1. **Identify Jobs-to-be-Done:**
   - What job is the customer hiring your product to do?
   - What are the functional, emotional, and social jobs?

2. **Quantify the value:**
   - What are the measurable outcomes?
   - Time saved, revenue gained, risk reduced?

3. **Write value proposition:**
   - For [target customer]
   - Who [has this problem/job]
   - [Product name] is a [category]
   - That [key benefit]
   - Unlike [competitor/alternative]
   - Our product [main differentiation]

### Step 3: Create Positioning Statement

Develop a positioning statement that guides all communications:

**Template:**
"For [target customer] who [has problem/job], [product name] is a [category] that [key benefit]. Unlike [competitor/alternative], [product name] [main differentiation]."

1. **Target customer:** Specific segment
2. **Problem/job:** The core problem you're solving
3. **Category:** How you compete (helps set expectations)
4. **Key benefit:** The primary value
5. **Differentiation:** Why you're different

### Step 4: Develop Key Messages

Create messages for different audiences and situations:

1. **Elevator pitch (30 seconds):**
   - One sentence that captures the essence
   - Can be understood by any audience

2. **Short description (2 minutes):**
   - What you do, for whom, why it matters
   - Includes differentiation

3. **Long description (5 minutes):**
   - Full context, features, benefits
   - For interested prospects

4. **Audience-specific messages:**
   - For developers (technical)
   - For executives (business value)
   - For end users (personal benefit)

### Step 5: Build Message Hierarchy

Organize messages by importance and context:

1. **Primary message:** The one thing you want to be known for
2. **Secondary messages:** Supporting points (2-3)
3. **Proof points:** Evidence supporting each message
4. **Objection responses:** Common pushback with rebuttals

### Step 6: Document in Framework

Synthesize into a usable messaging framework:

1. **Value proposition:** The core message
2. **Positioning statement:** The formal statement
3. **Key messages:** By audience and context
4. **Proof points:** Supporting evidence
5. **Messaging by channel:** How to adapt for different channels

## Output Format

The output follows the structure defined in [references/output-schema.md](references/output-schema.md):

- **Section 1:** Product Context — what, who, why
- **Section 2:** Value Proposition — the core message
- **Section 3:** Positioning Statement — formal statement
- **Section 4:** Key Messages — by audience
- **Section 5:** Proof Points — supporting evidence
- **Section 6:** Message by Channel — adaptation guidance
- **Section 7:** Objection Handling — common pushback

Expected length: 2,000-3,000 words depending on audience complexity.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Feature-focused messaging:** Talking about what the product does rather than what the customer gets. Always lead with value, not features.

2. **Generic positioning:** "Best-in-class," "innovative," "enterprise-grade" — these mean nothing. Be specific about what makes you different.

3. **One-size-fits-all:** Same message for developers and executives. Different audiences care about different things.

4. **No proof:** Making claims without evidence. Every message needs supporting proof points.

5. **Not testing:** Creating messaging without validating it resonates. Test with real customers before launching.
