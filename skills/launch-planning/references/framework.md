# Launch Planning Framework

## Overview

Launch planning is the coordination of all activities required to bring a product to market. This framework provides methodology for creating realistic, actionable launch timelines.

---

## Launch Types

### Full Public Launch
- Complete product release
- Maximum marketing push
- All stakeholders engaged
- Full support escalation ready

### Beta/Early Access
- Limited audience
- Feedback collection focus
- Iterative improvements
- Lower stakes

### Phased Rollout
- Gradual feature exposure
- Geographic or segment based
- Risk mitigation
- Learning-focused

### Re-launch/Update
- Existing user base focus
- Migration considerations
- Feature announcement
- Retention focus

---

## Phase Definitions

### Pre-Launch (4-6 weeks before)

| Activity Category | Typical Duration | Dependencies |
|-----------------|------------------|--------------|
| Product finalization | 2-4 weeks | Development complete |
| QA and testing | 2-3 weeks | Feature complete |
| Marketing assets | 2-4 weeks | Product finalized |
| Content creation | 2-3 weeks | Messaging complete |
| Internal enablement | 1-2 weeks | Content ready |
| List building | Ongoing | - |

### Launch Week

| Activity | Timing | Owner |
|----------|--------|-------|
| Deploy to production | Day 0 | Engineering |
| Monitor systems | Day 0-1 | Engineering |
| Launch announcement | Day 1 | Marketing |
| Outreach/press | Day 1-3 | Marketing |
| Support escalation | Week 1 | Support |
| Quick fixes | As needed | Engineering |

### Post-Launch (2-4 weeks)

- User feedback collection
- Issue monitoring and resolution
- Content follow-up
- Performance review
- Community management

### Stabilization (Ongoing)

- Bug fixes
- Performance optimization
- Feature iterations
- Scale planning

---

## Milestone Best Practices

### Definition
- Specific and measurable
- Has clear completion criteria
- Has owner accountable
- Has realistic deadline

### Dependencies
- Identify all dependencies
- Map dependency chain
- Build in buffer
- Re-assess regularly

### Tracking
- Daily standups for launch week
- Weekly reviews for pre-launch
- Clear status reporting
- Escalation path defined

---

## Go/No-Go Criteria

### Categories

| Category | Examples |
|----------|----------|
| Product | Critical bugs resolved, performance acceptable |
| Marketing | Assets ready, channels prepared |
| Technical | Systems stable, monitoring in place |
| Legal | Terms ready, compliance addressed |
| Support | Team trained, docs complete |

### Decision Framework

1. **List all criteria**
2. **Categorize as must-have vs nice-to-have**
3. **Assign owners**
4. **Track status**
5. **Make decision based on must-haves**

## Risk Management

### Common Launch Risks

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|------------|--------|------------|
| Delay | High | Major | Buffer time, prioritize ruthlessly |
| Bugs | High | Major | QA focus, feature flagging |
| Low turnout | Medium | Major | List building, pre-launch buzz |
| Tech issues | Medium | Major | Monitoring, rollbacks |
| Competition | Low | Major | Positioning, timing |

### Mitigation Strategies

1. **Build buffer** — Add 20% to timeline
2. **Prioritize ruthlessly** — Cut non-essentials
3. **Have rollback** — Know how to pull back
4. **Communicate early** — Warn stakeholders
5. **Monitor closely** — Watch for issues

## Contingency Planning

### Delay Scenario

**Trigger:** Go/no-go criteria not met

**Response:**
1. Identify specific issues
2. Assess delay duration
3. Communicate to stakeholders
4. Reschedule or reduce scope
5. Document learnings

### Rollback Scenario

**Trigger:** Critical bug or security issue

**Response:**
1. Identify severity
2. Communicate immediately
3. Deploy fix or rollback
4. Update timeline
5. Plan re-launch
