# Product Vision Framework

## What Makes a Strong Product Vision

A product vision articulates the future you are creating. It serves as a north star for all product decisions.

## Vision Components

### Vision Statement

The north star. Where you are going.

Characteristics:
- 3-5 year time horizon
- Inspiring and ambitious
- Clear and memorable
- Differentiating

Example structure:
"We believe that [future state] is possible through [our approach]."

### Mission Statement

Why you exist. The purpose.

Characteristics:
- 1-2 sentences
- Customer-focused (not product-focused)
- Action-oriented
- Time-bound

### Positioning Statement

How you win in the market.

Structure:
"For [target customer] who [need/problem], [product] is [category] that [key benefit]. Unlike [competitor], [key differentiator]."

### Core Values

The principles that guide decisions.

Choose 3-5 values that:
- Are actionable, not abstract
- Reflect genuine beliefs
- Guide difficult decisions

### Strategic Pillars

The focus areas that advance the vision.

Typically 3 areas where you will excel.

## Process

1. Start with the problem, not the solution
2. Consider the customer, not just the product
3. Be specific, not generic
4. Test with real people

## Common Mistakes

1. **Too product-focused** - "We build the best X" instead of "We help people achieve Y"
2. **Too generic** - "We want to make the world better" - could apply to anything
3. **Too complex** - If it takes a paragraph to explain, it's not clear
4. **No differentiation** - Could apply to any competitor
5. **Not inspiring** - Technically correct but no one cares
